Sometimes, it is possible to separate two- or threedimensional motion of a body into independent motions in perpendicular directions: (a) motion along $x$ is independent from the motion along $y$ for 2D geometry; (b) motion along $x$ is independent from the motion along $y$, which is independent from the motion along $z$; (c) motion along $x$ is independent from the motion in $y-z$-plane. In particular, this can be done for frictionless collisions from a plane ${ }^{2}$ : if the axis $x$ lies in the plane, and $y$ is perpendicular to it, you can study separately motion along $x$ and motion along $y$.
