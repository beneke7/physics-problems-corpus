**Problem 2. Water Vapor Condensation**

Consider a vertically oriented, cylindrical closed container with height, say, 20 cm. Assume that the container's walls and its contents have maintained a temperature of $T=1°\mathrm{C}$ for an extended time. The contents consist of a paper-thin layer of water covering the container's base and above it the saturated vapor of this water, nothing else. The side wall is thermally insulated, but the base and top are thin metal sheets with good thermal conductivity, whose temperature we can control from outside.

Using this capability, we raise the temperature of the top to $T_f=100°\mathrm{C}$ while keeping the base temperature at $T=1°\mathrm{C}$, and ensure these values remain constant for a sufficient time. We wait until the water and its vapor in the container reach a new steady state that no longer changes.

**a)** Compared to the previous equilibrium state, has the number of water molecules in the vapor phase changed significantly, and if so, has it increased or decreased?

**b)** What would be the answer if initially the water layer had a height of 10 cm instead?

(Károlyházy Frigyes)
