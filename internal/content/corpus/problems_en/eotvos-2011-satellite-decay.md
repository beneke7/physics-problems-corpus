**Problem 1. Satellite Orbital Decay**

Abandoned satellites at the end of their operational life gradually lose their mechanical energy due to drag force proportional to the square of velocity, and eventually burn up when entering denser atmospheric layers. It can be shown that satellites originally in circular orbits, while approaching Earth's surface, remain approximately in circular orbits with slowly decreasing radius.

Suppose a satellite of mass $m=500\ \mathrm{kg}$, orbiting in the equatorial plane at altitude $h=400\ \mathrm{km}$, is abandoned! The drag force on the satellite can be expressed as $F_{\text{drag}}=K\varrho v^{2}$, where $K=0.23\ \mathrm{m}^2$, $\varrho$ is the air density at the satellite's altitude, and $v$ is the satellite's velocity.

**a)** Determine the change in the satellite's velocity as its orbital altitude decreases by half $(h \rightarrow h/2)$.

**b)** Between the drag force and the tangential component of the resultant of the two forces acting on the satellite (gravitational and drag), a simple relationship can be established. What is it?

**c)** What is the air density at altitude $h/2=200\ \mathrm{km}$ if the orbital radius decreases by $100\ \mathrm{m}$ per revolution at this altitude?

Data required for the solution can be taken from tables.

(Honyek Gyula)
