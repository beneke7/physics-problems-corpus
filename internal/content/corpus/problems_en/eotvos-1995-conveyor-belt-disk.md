**Problem 1. Disk on a Moving Conveyor Belt.**

A square experimental table of width $l=3$ m has a flat surface; however, its central strip of width $d=1$ m is formed by an (infinite) conveyor belt moving at a constant speed $v=3$ m/s, which fits exactly with the level of the rest of the table surface. We place a small, flat disk at the middle of one edge of the table (at point $A$ shown in figure 1), and strike it so that it begins to slide at $u=4$ m/s (perpendicular) toward the belt. The friction between the stationary part of the table and the disk is negligible, while the coefficient of friction between the conveyor belt and the disk is $\mu=0.5$.

**Where does the disk fall off the table?**
