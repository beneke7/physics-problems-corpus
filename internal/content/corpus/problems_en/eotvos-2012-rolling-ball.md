**1. Rolling ball on a rotating disk.**

A flat, rough disk is inclined at an angle $\alpha$ to the horizontal and rotates uniformly with angular velocity $\Omega$. A magician places a solid rubber ball of radius $R$ at the center of the rotating disk and rolls it in an appropriate direction. To the audience's great amazement, the center of the ball subsequently moves in a straight line with constant velocity, continuing this motion until the ball reaches the edge of the rotating disk. (The ball rolls purely throughout, and the disk's angular velocity remains constant.)

Provide a physical explanation for this curious phenomenon! In what direction and with what initial conditions must the magician launch the ball for the trick to succeed?
