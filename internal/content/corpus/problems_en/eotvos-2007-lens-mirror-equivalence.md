A teacher poses the following problem to his gifted students: Investigate theoretically whether an optical system consisting of a thin converging lens and a plane mirror parallel to it can be replaced by a single concave mirror!

Anna examines a case where the converging lens has a focal length $f$ = 30 cm, and the lens is positioned $\ell=20 \mathrm{~cm}$ in front of the mirror. By cleverly choosing object distances, she is able to determine the focal length $f^{*}$ of the required concave mirror and its distance $x$ from the position of the lens.

Balázs wants to solve the problem in general, and does not rest until he finds expressions that give $f^{*}$ and $x$ as functions of $f$ and $\ell$.

Cecilia finally recognizes that the optical system cannot be replaced by a concave mirror for all pairs of $(f, \ell)$ values, so she thinks through what condition must be satisfied for Balázs's solution to be valid.

Follow the work of Anna, Balázs, and Cecilia! How do they solve their respective tasks?
