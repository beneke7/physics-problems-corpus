**3. Problem. Superconducting Loops Around a Solenoid.**

A direct current is maintained in a long, narrow solenoid. For example, let the length of the coil be $\ell = 60$ cm, its radius $r = 2$ cm, the number of turns $N = 600$, and the current $I_0 = 1$ mA.

Around the middle of the coil we wrap a simple closed conducting loop (A) with no gap, and at the mouth of the solenoid we place an identical-diameter but double loop (a closed "two-turn coil") (B), as shown in Figure 5. Both A and B are made of a material that can relatively easily be made superconducting, with its ohmic resistance becoming zero at a sufficiently low temperature.

Initially, of course, no current flows in A and B. But now we cool them down, make them superconducting, and then break the circuit of the solenoid. Then (since the magnetic flux passing through a closed superconducting circuit cannot change), a current $I_A$ is induced in loop A and a current $I_B$ in the double loop, and these currents persist.

1. Compare the magnitudes of $I_A$ and $I_B$! Are they approximately equal, and if not, which is larger and by what factor?
2. Using the data for the solenoid, provide a reasonable estimate for the value of $I_A$!

*(Frigyes Károlyházy)*
