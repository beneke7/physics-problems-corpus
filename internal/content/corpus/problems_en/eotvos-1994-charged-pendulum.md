**3. Problem. Charged Pendulum Near a Metal Plate.**

At a distance $d$ from a vertical grounded metal plate, we suspend a pendulum of length $l$. After electrically charging a small pendulum bob of mass $m$, the pendulum reaches a new equilibrium position, now making an angle $\alpha$ with the vertical.

| What is the charge on the pendulum bob?
| By how much must we move the metal plate closer to the point of suspension if we want the vertical metal plate to attract the pendulum bob to itself?
| Without moving the plate closer, can we move the always-vertical metal plate in such a way that the bob collides with it?

The string is an insulator, air resistance is negligible, and solve the problem with the following numerical values: $d=0.5 \mathrm{~m} ; l=4 \mathrm{~m} ; m=10^{-3} \mathrm{~kg} ; \alpha=1^{\circ}$.

*Gyula Radnai*
