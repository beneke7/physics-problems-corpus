**3. Solenoid and suspended conducting frame**

The cross-section of a solenoid (long, straight coil) is a square of side $d$,
while its length is $L$ ($L \gg d$). Deep inside the solenoid, a homogeneous
magnetic field of induction $B_0$ is built up, due to the direct current
flowing in the coil. The coil is in a vertical position. Right above the top
end of the coil, a conducting frame of the form of a horizontal square with
sides $d$ is suspended on vertical threads of length $l$ ($l \gg d$). The mass
of the frame is $m$, its electrical resistance is $R$. The solenoid is suddenly
pulled to the right horizontally. In what direction does the frame, suspended as
a pendulum, swing out and how high does it rise?
