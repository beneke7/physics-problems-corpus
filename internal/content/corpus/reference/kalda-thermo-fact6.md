The heat exchange rate (i.e. the heat flux, measured in Watts) between a body and the environment is a function of the temperatures of the body and of the environment, $T_{1}$ and $T_{2}$, respectively; for a small tempera difference $\Delta T \equiv T_{1}-T_{2}$, the heat flux is proportional to $\Delta T$; this is referred to as the Fourier's law. For larger temperature differences, the dependence becomes nonlinear, because (a) heat conductivity of the materials may depend on the temperature, (b) the heat flux due to heat radiation is a non-linear function of $T_{1}$ and $T_{2}$ (however, it can be still linearized for small values of $\Delta T$ ); (c) large temperature differences may cause convection of air and fluids which will enhance heat flux in a nonlinear way. Pay attention that the heat flux goes to zero for $T_{1}=T_{2}$ !
