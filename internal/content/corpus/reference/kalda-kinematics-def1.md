A scalar quantity is a quantity which can be fully described by a single numerical value only; a vector quantity is a quantity which needs to be described by a magnitude (also referred to as modulus or length), and a direction. The sum of two vectors $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$ is defined so that if the vectors are interpreted as displacements (the modulus of a vector gives the distance, and its direction - the direction of the displacement) then the vector $\vec{a}+\vec{b}$ corresponds to the net displacement as a result of two sequentially performed displacements $\vec{a}$ and $\vec{b}$.
