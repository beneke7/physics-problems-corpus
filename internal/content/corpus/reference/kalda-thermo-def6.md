An ideal gas is made up of molecules of negligible size (as compared with the average intermolecular distances) which behave as elastic balls (monoatomic gas) or spring-ballsystems (polyatomic gas), and move randomly colliding with each other and with the walls of the container. It is assumed that there are no other forces between the molecules than the contact forces (for instance, electrostatic interactions need to be negligible as electrostatic forces act over a distance).
