Using the assumptions and numerical data of problem 3, and the density of tungsten at room temperature $\rho=19250 \mathrm{~kg} / \mathrm{m}^{3}$, answer the following questions. a) A smaller-than-nominal direct voltage $U_{0}=3 \mathrm{~V}$ is applied to the leads of the halogen bulb. How long does it take for the tungsten filament to reach temperature $T_{1}=40^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ starting from the room temperature $T_{r}=20^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ ?
b) Accidentally, a too large DC voltage $U_{1}=120 \mathrm{~V}$ is applied to the leads of the lamp. How long does it take to reach the melting temperature $T_{2}=3410^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ of the tungsten? Heat losses can be neglected, as well as the temperature dependence of the tungsten's density and specific heat; use the graph depicting the resistivity of tungsten as a function of temperature.
