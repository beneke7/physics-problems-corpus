The two thinnest strings of a steel-string guitar (E and B) are made of great tensile strength steel. The thinner E string is overstrained until its pitch reaches sound G (a whole tone below the 440 Hz normal A sound) and it snaps.
 $a)$ At which sound would string B snap if it was also overstrained and it was made of the same tensile strength steel as string E?
 $b)$ What was the tensile strength of the material of string E?
 $c)$ The strings which have been used for a long time usually break at the bridge or at the tuning peg. Why?
 $d)$ From what other materials could the strings be made? Look for appropriate materials on the Internet or in tables.
 The length of the oscillating part of the string is 64 cm, the density of the material of the steel string is $7.8 \cdot 10^3~\rm kg/m^3$.
 (6 pont)
