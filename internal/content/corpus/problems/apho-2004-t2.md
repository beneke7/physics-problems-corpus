Theoretical Competition-Problem No. 2

## Optical fiber

![](apho-2004-t2-fig01.png)
![](apho-2004-t2-fig02.png)

An optical fiber consists of a cylindrical core of radius $a$, made of a transparent material with refraction index varying gradually from the value $n=n_{1}$ on the axis to $n=n_{2}$ (with $1<n_{2}<n_{1}$ ) at a distance $a$ from the axis, according to the formula

$$
n=n(x)=n_{1} \sqrt{1-\alpha^{2} x^{2}}
$$

where $x$ is the distance from the core axis and $\alpha$ is a constant. The core is surrounded by a cladding made of a material with constant refraction index $n_{2}$. Outside the fiber is air, of refractive index $n_{0}$.

Let $\mathrm{O} z$ be the axis of the fiber, with O - the center of the fiber end.
Given $n_{0}=1.000 ; n_{1}=1.500 ; n_{2}=1.460, a=25 \mu \mathrm{~m}$.

1. A monochromatic light ray enters the fiber at point O under an incident angle $\theta_{\mathrm{i}}$, the incident plane being the plane $x \mathrm{O} z$.
a. Show that at each point on the trajectory of the light in the fiber, the refractive index $n$ and the angle $\theta$ between the light ray and the $\mathrm{O} z$ axis satisfy the relationship $n \boldsymbol{\operatorname { c o s }} \theta=C$ where $C$ is a constant. Find the expression for $C$ in terms of $n_{1}$ and $\theta_{\mathrm{i}}$. [1.0 points]
b. Use the result found in 1.a. and the trigonometric relation $\boldsymbol{\operatorname { c o s }} \theta=\left(1+\boldsymbol{\operatorname { t a n }}^{2} \theta\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}$, where $\boldsymbol{\operatorname { t a n }} \theta=\frac{d x}{d z}=x^{\prime}$ is the slope of the tangent to the trajectory at point ( $x, z$ ), derive an equation for $x^{\prime}$. Find the full expression for $\alpha$ in terms of $n_{1}, n_{2}$ and $a$. By differentiating the two sides of this equation versus $z$, find the equation for the second derivative $x^{\prime \prime}$. [1.0 points]
c. Find the expression of $x$ as a function of $z$, that is $x=f(z)$, which satisfies the above equation. This is the equation of the trajectory of light in the fiber. [1.0 points]
d. Sketch one full period of the trajectories of the light rays entering the fiber under two different incident angles $\theta_{\mathrm{i}}$. [1.0 points]
2. Light propagates in the optical fiber.
a. Find the maximum incident angle $\theta_{\mathrm{iM}}$, under which the light ray still can propagate inside the core of the fiber. [1.5 points]
b. Determine the expression for coordinate $z$ of the crossing points of a light ray with $\mathrm{O} z$ axis for $\theta_{\mathrm{i}} \neq 0$. [1.5 points]
3. The light is used to transmit signals in the form of very short light pulses (of negligible pulse width).
a. Determine the time $\tau$ it takes the light to travel from point O to the first crossing point with $\mathrm{O} z$ for incident angle $\theta_{\mathrm{i}} \neq 0$ and $\theta_{\mathrm{i}} \leq \theta_{\mathrm{iM}}$.

The ratio of the coordinate $z$ of the first crossing point and $\tau$ is called the propagation speed of the light signal along the fiber. Assume that this speed varies monotonously with $\theta_{i}$.

Find this speed (called $v_{\mathrm{M}}$ ) for $\theta_{\mathrm{i}}=\theta_{\mathrm{iM}}$.
Find also the propagation speed (called $v_{0}$ ) of the light along the axis Oz.
Compare the two speeds. [3.25 points]
b. The light beam bearing the signals is a converging beam entering the fiber at O under different incident angles $\theta_{i}$ with $0 \leq \theta_{i} \leq \theta_{\mathrm{iM}}$. Calculate the highest repetition frequency $f$ of the signal pulses, so that at a distance $z=1000 \mathrm{~m}$ two consecutive pulses are still separated (that is, the pulses do not overlap). [1.75 points]

Attention

1. The wave properties of the light are not considered in this problem.
2. Neglect any chromatic dispersion in the fiber.
3. The speed of light in vacuum is $\mathrm{c}=2.998 \times 10^{8} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
4. You may use the following formulae:
- The length of a small arc element $d s$ in the $x \mathrm{O} z$ plane is
$$
d s=d z \sqrt{1+\left(\frac{d x}{d z}\right)^{2}}
$$
![](apho-2004-t2-fig03.png)
$$
\text { - } \int \frac{d x}{\sqrt{a^{2}-b^{2} x^{2}}}=\frac{1}{b} \operatorname{Arcsin} \frac{b x}{a}
$$
$$
\text { - } \int \frac{x^{2} d x}{\sqrt{a^{2}-b^{2} x^{2}}}=-\frac{x \sqrt{a^{2}-b^{2} x^{2}}}{2 b^{2}}+\frac{a^{2} \operatorname{Arcsin} \frac{b x}{a}}{2 b^{3}}
$$

- $\boldsymbol{\operatorname { A r c s i n }} x$ is the inverse function of the sine function. Its value equals the less angle the sine of which is $x$. In other words, if $y=\boldsymbol{\operatorname { A r c s i n }} x$ then $\boldsymbol{\operatorname { s i n }} y=x$.
