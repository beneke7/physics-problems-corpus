Q7
(a) The abundances of $\mathrm{U}^{238}$ and $\mathrm{U}^{235}$ are in the ratio, respectively, of 140:1. Equal amounts of each isotope existed in the Earth's crust at its formation. Estimate the age of the Earth. The half-lives are: $\mathrm{U}^{238} 4.5 \times 10^{9}$ years, $\mathrm{U}^{235} 7.1 \times 10^{8}$ years.
[6]
(b) A steel compression ring for the piston of a car is irradiated with neutrons until it has a uniformly distributed activity of $4 \times 10^{5} \mathrm{~Bq}$ due to the formation of $\mathrm{Fe}^{59}$. The ring is immediately installed in the engine. After the engine has been running for 30 days, a $100 \mathrm{~cm}^{3}$ of the engine oil is taken out and 126 disintegrations are recorded from it during a 10 min counting period. If the total volume of the oil is $5.0 \times 10^{-3} \mathrm{~m}^{3}$, what fraction of the ring has worn away during the running period? Assume all the metal worn away is in suspension in the oil.
$$
\text { (1 Bq is one disintegration per sec., half-life } \mathrm{Fe}^{59}=45 \text { days) }
$$
(c) A radioactive detector is used to measure the count rate of a single radioactive source. It initially registered 82 counts $\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, which dropped after 210 s to 19 counts $\mathrm{s}^{-1}$. The halflife of the substance was 70 s. Verify that the count rate does not satisfy an exponential decay law. A constant background radiation was present during the measurements. Determine its count rate.
(d) If in (c) the anomaly was due to the presence of a radioactive substance, with a much greater half life than the source, how would one deduce its half life from experimental measurements?
