**7. BOUNCY BALL (5 points)** — *J. Toots*

A homogeneous elastic ball with radius $R$ bounces from a vertical wall in such a way that it rebounds back exactly in the same direction it came from. The ball's speed before the bounce is $v$ at an angle $\alpha$ to the vertical; the ball also has an angular speed $\omega$. There was no slipping at the contact point, but do not assume that $\omega R=v \cos \alpha$. The bounce was completely elastic, ie no kinetic energy was lost; also, the horizontal components of incident and rebound velocities have the same magnitude. The moment of inertia for a homogeneous ball is $I=\frac{2}{5} m R^{2}$.

i) *(1 point)* Find the angular speed $\omega_{2}$ of the ball after the bounce.

ii) *(2 points)* Find the initial angular speed $\omega$ in terms of $v, \alpha$ and $R$.

iii) *(2 points)* What is the minimum value for coefficient of friction $\mu$ that such bounce could happen?
