**6. MAGNETS (6 points)**

Certain types of magnetic toys are made up of ferromagnetic spheres and permanent
magnets of cylindrical shape. These building blocks can be used to build, for
instance, a tetrahedron; see the figure (the letter “N” marks the northern end of a
magnet). Assume that all these permanent magnets are identical and each of them
alone can create a magnetic flux $\Phi$ (assuming both ends of the magnet are in
contact with a large U-shaped piece of ferromagnetic material, so that a closed
ferromagnetic contour is formed). Assume also that, due to the high magnetic
permeability of the material of the building blocks, all the magnetic field lines are
constrained inside them (i.e. in the surrounding medium, the magnetic induction
$B=0$).

![figure](../../figures/nbpho/figures/nbpho-2004-magnets-fig1.png)

**1)** Let us denote the fluxes in the permanent magnets (magnets A–F in the figure)
by $\Phi_A,\ldots,\Phi_F$. Write down an equation relating $\Phi_A$, $\Phi_B$, and
$\Phi_C$ to each other (and possibly to $\Phi$) (1 point).

**2)** Write down an equation relating $\Phi_A$, $\Phi_B$, and $\Phi_F$ to each
other (and possibly to $\Phi$) (1 point).

**3)** Find the ratio $\Phi_F/\Phi_C$ (1 point).

**4)** Find the magnetic fluxes in each permanent magnet (2 points).

**5)** Which of the magnets is the most difficult one to remove? Motivate your answer
(1 point).
