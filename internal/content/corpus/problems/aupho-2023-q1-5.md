1. To determine the location of the rain, the radar uses time of flight. Because the pulse travels at the speed of light $\left(3.00 \times 10^{8} \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}\right)$, the time between when the pulse is emitted and received back by the radar can be used to determine the distance from the radar to the rain.

![](aupho-2023-q1-5-fig01.png)
The time between when a pulse is emitted and returned is 0.32 ms. How far away is the rain from the radar? (1 mark)
![](aupho-2023-q1-5-fig02.png)
When the pulse encounters an interface, some radiation is reflected and some is transmitted. Every material has a refractive index (n), which is the ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum to the speed in the material. The proportion of light which is reflected depends both on the refractive indices of the materials on either side of the interface and the angle the light is incident. Assuming the light is incident normal to the surface, the proportion of light which is reflected can be calculated with the equation:

$$
R=\frac{I_{R}}{I_{0}}=\left(\frac{n_{2}-n_{1}}{n_{2}+n_{1}}\right)^{2} .
$$
