## Question 6

a) Sea waves of frequency $f$ are moving North with velocity $c$. A small boat moves South with a velocity $u$, both velocities being measured with respect to the land. By counting the number of waves moving past the boat in a time $\Delta t$ or otherwise, show that the boat will be moving up and down with a frequency $f^{\prime}$ given by
$$
f^{\prime}=\frac{(u+c) f}{c}
$$
b) (i) A source of sound, emitting a single note of frequency $f$ is moving towards a stationary observer with speed $v_{\mathrm{s}}$. Show that if the air is stationary, the frequency $f^{\prime}$ detected by the observer is given by
$$
f^{\prime}=\frac{f}{\left(1-\frac{v_{\mathrm{s}}}{c}\right)}
$$
where $c$ is the speed of sound in air. You may wish to consider a time $\Delta t$, and how many waves are emitted by the source, how far the waves and the source each move in this time, the wavelength the observer then detects, or any other approach.
    (ii) What would be the value of $\Delta f=f^{\prime}-f$ if the source is moving away from the observer at a speed $v_{\mathrm{s}}=c / 2$ ?
    (iii) If $v_{\mathrm{s}} \ll c$, show that $\frac{\Delta f}{f} \approx \frac{v_{\mathrm{s}}}{c}$
c) The above formula can be applied to electromagnetic waves.
    (i) Show that the observed frequency of an absorption line of emitted frequency $f$ in the spectrum of the Sun should vary between $f+\delta f$ and $f-\delta f$ where
$$
\delta f=\frac{2 \pi R f}{T c}
$$
in which $R$ is the radius of the Sun, and $T$ the period of rotation.
    (ii) Given that the density of the Sun is $1.4 \times 10^{3} \mathrm{~kg} \mathrm{~m}^{-3}$, the mass is $2.0 \times 10^{30} \mathrm{~kg}$ and the equatorial rotation period is 24.5 days, estimate the fractional frequency shift, $\frac{\delta f}{f}$, for the peak in the visible spectrum at 550 nm.

d) Two stars labelled A and B separated by a distance $a=19.8 \mathrm{AU}$ form a binary pair, gravitationally bound, orbiting a common centre of mass with a period $T=50.1 \mathrm{y}$. Star A, with mass $m_{\mathrm{A}}$ is at radius $R_{\mathrm{A}}$ from the centre of mass, and star B, a very faint white dwarf, of mass $m_{\mathrm{B}}$ orbits at radius $R_{\mathrm{B}}$. The stars have circular orbits with the plane of the orbit lying along the line of sight from the Earth. Only the brightest of the two stars can be observed through a telescope, and it is found that a spectral line of 656.3 nm in the lab varies by ±0.0235 nm when observed in the bright star.
    (i) Show that $T^{2}=\frac{4 \pi^{2}}{G M} a^{3}$ where $M=m_{\mathrm{A}}+m_{\mathrm{B}}$ is the total mass of the binary pair.
    (ii) From the figures given, calculate a value for the total mass of the system.
    (iii) Show that $v_{\mathrm{A}}=\frac{G T}{2 \pi a^{2}} m_{\mathrm{B}}$ where $v_{\mathrm{A}}$ is the orbital velocity of star A.
    (iv) From the spectral line shift, calculate mass $m_{\mathrm{B}}$ and hence the mass of star $\mathrm{A}, m_{\mathrm{A}}$.
    (v) Hence calculate the ratio of the orbital radii, $\frac{R_{\mathrm{A}}}{R_{\mathrm{B}}}$.
$$
1 \mathrm{AU}=1.50 \times 10^{11} \mathrm{~m}
$$
