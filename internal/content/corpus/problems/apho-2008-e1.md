## PART 1. Characteristics of the microphone system ( 3 points )

## Introduction

The property, called piezoelectricity, provides a convenient coupling between mechanical oscillations of crystal, which occur at a very sharply defined frequency, and the electrical properties of a circuit of which the crystal is a part. Piezoelectric materials are used to convert electric and sound signals into each other. But they are distinguished by having a well specified working frequency range. Therefore, in this experimental part, we have to determine physical properties of the microphone which is made of piezoelectric material, before using it.

## PART 2. Interference of Waves ( 6 points )

## Introduction

A standing wave pattern is a vibrational pattern created within a medium when the reflected waves from mirror to interfere with incident waves from the source. The waves are interfering in such a manner that there are points of no displacement produced at the same positions along the medium. These points along the medium are known as nodes. There are other points along the medium which undergo vibrations between a large positive and large negative displacement. These points are known as antinodes.

## PART 3. The Doppler effect ( 8 points )

The observed and measured frequency of a signal changes by virtue of relative motion between the source and the observer. This is known as the Doppler effect.

The observed frequency is given by the formulas

$$
\begin{array}{ll}
f=f_{o} \frac{c-v_{d}}{c+v_{s}} & \text { (Source and Detector receding) } \\
f=f_{o} \frac{c+v_{d}}{c-v_{s}} & \text { (Source and Detector approaching) }
\end{array}
$$

where
$f_{o}$ frequency of the wave emitted by the Source

$c$ speed of sound in air
$v_{d}$ velocity of the Detector
$v_{s}$ velocity of the Source

## PART 4: Threshold and Resolving Power of Hearing ( 3 points )

The threshold of hearing is the sound intensity in $\mathrm{W} / \mathrm{m}^{2}$ at which our ear can barely hear it. This threshold depends on sound frequency. The threshold of hearing at about 2000 Hz is equal to $10^{-12} \mathrm{~W} / \mathrm{m}^{2}$. The ratio $\log _{10}(\mathrm{I} / \mathrm{Io})$ is called as sound intensity level and is measured in units of Bell, abbreviated as B . In the practice it is more convenient to use the ratio $\beta$ (in dB) $=10 \log _{10}(\mathrm{I} / \mathrm{Io})$. The human ear can hear sounds from about 20 Hz to about 20,000 Hz. It called that the audible range.

4b. The frequency region to be heard the best ( 1 point )

1. Lower the sound intensity step by step. For each step find the $\boldsymbol{f}_{\text {low }}$ and $\boldsymbol{f}_{\text {high }}$. Find out a region where you can hear lowest level of sound intensity, as possible as can. The lower limit of this region is lowest frequency of the frequency region to be heard the best, $\boldsymbol{f}_{t h 1}$; and upper limit of this region is the highest frequency of the frequency region to be heard the best, $\boldsymbol{f}_{t h 2}$.
Average of this two limits or the most sensitive frequency to be heard is

$$
f_{t h}=\frac{f_{t h 1}+f_{t h 2}}{2}
$$

## 4c. Resolving power ( $\boldsymbol{R}$ ) of ear for different frequencies of sound. ( $\mathbf{1 . 0}$ point)

Resolving power of ear is an ability to distinguish two close frequencies.

1. Set the frequency of the generator to $f_{t h}$.
2. While hearing with the headphone, change the intensity to the level that you can hear well.
3. Change the frequency a little. If you can not hear a difference between $f_{t h}$ and the changed frequency, make the difference a little more. Find a frequency that you can hear as a different from the $f_{t h}$. Difference between the $f_{t h}$ and the changed frequency is resolveable frequency of your ear at $f_{t h}$.
4. Calculate resolveable frequency ( $\Delta f$ ) and resolving power $R=f / \Delta f$ for the $f_{t h}$.
