## Q10

a) An insulated closed vessel contains a block of ice at $-20^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$. A constant wattage heater and a temperature sensor are embedded in the ice. The heater is switched on and the system slowly heats up so that it is always at a uniform temperature, at all times.
Sketch graphs showing the variation, from $-20^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ to $120^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$, of the following:
(i) temperature, T , with time, t
(ii) density, $\rho$, with temperature, T

Explain the arrangement and motions of the molecules in the different temperature regions.
b) A Dewar flask contains a liquefied gas into which has been inserted an electrical heater. The heater has a resistance of $2.00 \Omega$ and takes a current of 0.90 A . The flask rests on a balance. The balance reading is recorded at two minute intervals, before and after the heater is switched on. The table below gives the recorded results.

Determine the specific heat of vaporization of the liquid.

| Time/minutes | 0.00 | 2.00 | 4.00 | 6.00 | 8.00 | 10.00 | 12.00 | 14.00 | 16.00 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mass/grams | 73.0 | 72.5 | 72.0 | 71.5 | 70.5 | 68.5 | 66.5 | 64.5 | 62.5 |

c) Black coffee at $70.0^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$, with a mass of 225 grams and specific heat capacity of $4200 \mathrm{~J} \mathrm{~kg}^{-1}{ }^{\circ} \mathrm{C}^{-1}$, is poured into a porcelain mug which is at $20.0^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$, with mass 200 grams and specific heat capacity of $1200 \mathrm{~J} \mathrm{~kg}^{-1}{ }^{\circ} \mathrm{C}^{-1}$. This is followed by the addition of 15 grams of cream at $5.0^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$, with specific heat capacity of $4000 \mathrm{~J} \mathrm{~kg}^{-1}{ }^{\circ} \mathrm{C}^{-1}$. Determine the final equilibrium temperature of the white coffee assuming no heat is lost.

Explain, without any calculation, why more black coffee, at $70^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$, is required to raise the equilibrium temperature of the white coffee by $1^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ when it is at temperature $\mathrm{T}_{1}{ }^{\circ} \mathrm{C}$ compared with that at temperature $\mathrm{T}_{2}{ }^{\circ} \mathrm{C}, \mathrm{T}_{1}$ being greater than $\mathrm{T}_{2}$.
