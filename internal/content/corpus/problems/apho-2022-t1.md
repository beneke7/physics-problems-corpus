## The Stern-Gerlach Experiment ${ }^{1}$

The Stern-Gerlach experiment was performed in 1922 and it eventually led to the determination of the magnetic moment of the electron. In this experiment a beam of silver atoms each of mass $m=$ $1.80 \times 10^{-25} \mathrm{~kg}$ emerges from an oven kept at temperature $\mathrm{T}=1.20 \times 10^{3} \mathrm{~K}$ (see Figure 1). Assume that on emerging from the oven all the atoms have the same momentum along the direction of the beam ( $z$-direction). Ignore gravity.

![](apho-2022-t1-fig01.png)
Figure 1: Schematic diagram of the Stern-Gerlach setup.

A. 1 Speed of the Silver Atoms: The speed $v_{z}$ of the silver atoms emerging from 0.5pt the oven can be estimated to be $\sqrt{3 k_{B} T / m}$ using the equipartition theorem. Calculate this value.

## Q1-2 <br> Official (English)

B. 1 The Basic Expression: After emerging from the oven the silver atoms move along the $z$ - direction over a distance $l_{1}=0.25 \mathrm{~m}$. Next, the silver atoms pass between two magnets over a distance $l_{2}=0.5 \mathrm{~m}$. The magnets produce inhomogeneous magnetic field $B$ in the $x$-direction with constant gradient $d B / d x$. Assume that the silver atom has a magnetic moment pointing either in $+x$ direction or - $x$-direction, i.e. $\vec{\mu}_{s}= \pm \mu_{s} \hat{\imath}$. After passing through the magnets the silver atoms pass through a further distance $l_{3}=0.25 \mathrm{~m}$ before striking the screen $P P^{\prime}$. The distance between the two striking beams on the screen is $\Delta x$. Derive the expression for the splitting distance $\Delta x$ on the screen.

The Inhomogeneous Magnetic Field:
This part is concerned with the setup to create the inhomogenous magnetic field $(d B / d x \neq 0)$. It consists of a number of sub-parts. Two very long wires parallel to the $z$-axis carry currents of magnitude $I_{0}$ and are located at $A_{1}(0,-a, z)$ and $A_{2}(0, a, z)$ (see figure below). The direction of the current passing through $y=-a$ is $-\hat{k}$ and for the one passing through $y=a$ is $\hat{k}$. The entire system is inside a medium of high relative magnetic permeability $\mu_{r}$. We take $\mu=\mu_{0} \mu_{r}$. The wires are insulated and no current leaks into the medium.
