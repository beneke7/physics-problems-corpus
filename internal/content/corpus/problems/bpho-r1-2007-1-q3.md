Q3
(a) A double star consists of two stars, each with the same mass as our Sun, $M_{S}$, separated by a distance $d$. They are observed to complete a full rotation about their centre of mass in one week. Determine, to two significant figures, the ratio ( $d / R_{S E}$ ), where $R_{S E}$ is the Sun-Earth distance, without assuming the numerical value of $M_{S}$.
(b) In this calculation light may be considered as a stream of particles, photons, each having a mass ( $\mathrm{h} / \lambda \mathrm{c}$ ) and energy ( $\mathrm{hc} / \lambda$ ), where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the light. Light with $\lambda=500 \mathrm{~nm}$ is emitted from the surface of the Sun, radius $R_{\mathrm{S}}$, and received on Earth, radius $R_{E}$, slightly shifted in wavelength by $\Delta \lambda$ after travelling a distance $R_{S E}$.
(i) Give an algebraic expression for the change in the gravitational potential energy of the photon, $\Delta U$.
(ii) Estimate the relative order of magnitude of each term in the expression for $\Delta U$ in (i). Indicate which term/s can be neglected for the result to be correct to 2 significant figures.
(iii) Calculate $(\Delta \lambda / \lambda)$ to 2 significant figures.

These calculations give identical results to those involving a more rigorous calculation.
(c) Explain why the value of g at the Earth's equator differs from its value at the poles. Which has the greater value?
