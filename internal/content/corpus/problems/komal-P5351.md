Why is it not allowed to look into a laser light?
 The lens in the human eye can focus the light rays into a very small spot of diameter of several $\mu$m. The most sensitive cells are in the retina, and here the size of the cells so called rods and cones is in the range of $\mu$m. The power of lasers used in everyday life is between 0.1 mW and 100 mW.
 Calculate how long it takes for the light of the smallest laser, i.e. 0.1 mW, with 80% light absorption, to heat a cell to $50\,{}^\circ$C at which the cell gets damaged, and how long it takes to heat the cell to a temperature of $100\,{}^\circ$C at which the cell is totally destroyed. For the sake of simplicity consider the cell as a cylinder which has a base diameter of $5~\mu$m and a height of $7~\mu$m; the density and the specific heat capacity of the cell can be assumed to be the same as those of water. The temperature of the eye can be considered as $36\,{}^\circ$C, and other effects (as displacements, heat conduction etc.) can be neglected. Compare the gained time with the approximately 0.2 second reaction time of the human eye.
