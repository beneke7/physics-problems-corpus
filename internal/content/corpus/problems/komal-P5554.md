A glass of base area $20~\text{cm}^2$ contains $120~\text{g}$ water at a temperature of $25~^\circ\text{C}$. Put in an ice cube which has a temperature of $0~^\circ\text{C}$. When the ice cube melts completely, the water cools down to exactly $0~^\circ\text{C}$. The water equivalent of the glass is $40~\text{g}$, i.e. the heat capacity of the glass is equal to that of a sample of $40~\text{g}$ water. Ignore other heat losses and the thermal expansion of the glass, but consider the temperature dependence of the density of water. 

 $a)$ What was the mass of the ice cube? 

 $b)$ How much does the level of the water in the glass change during the melting process of the ice? 
 (4 pont)
