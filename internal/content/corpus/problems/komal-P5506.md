Consider the electron as a small, uniform-density, and uniformly charged sphere such that its energy calculated from the mass-energy equivalence equation is equal to the energy of the electrostatic field around the electron. 
 $a)$ Determine the radius of the electron, which is called the classical radius of the electron. 
 $b)$ The electron is a particle with half-integer spin, because its intrinsic angular momentum is half of the reduced Planck constant $h/2\pi$, that is, $\hbar/2$. Let us consider the electron's intrinsic angular momentum according to classical Newtonian mechanics, as the angular momentum of a uniform solid sphere rotating about an axis passing through its centre. Determine the tangential speed of a point on the ``equator'' of the rotating classical electron. Compare this with the speed of light.
