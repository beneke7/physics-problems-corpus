A4. An atom in an excited state typically decays by emitting a photon. The atom is originally in some excited state $S^{\prime}$, and after the decay it is in the ground state $S$. We usually think of the photon as carrying away the energy difference between states $S^{\prime}$ and $S$. In this problem we examine this process closely.
(a, 4) Consider a stationary, isolated atom in some excited state $S^{\prime}$. Before decay the mass of the atom is $M^{\prime}$. The atom decays into the ground state $S$. In the ground state the mass of the atom is $M$. Thus the energy $\left(M^{\prime}-M\right) c^{2}$, which we denote here as $E_{o}$, is the energy that is released by the atom, and is therefore an upper limit on the energy that the photon could possibly carry away ( $c$ is the speed of light). Let $E$ denote the energy of the outgoing photon. Without solving any equation, by merely giving an qualitative argument, explain why $E$ must be strictly less than $E_{o}$ in the case of an isolated excited atom undergoing radiative decay.
$(\mathbf{b}, \mathbf{6})$ Quantify your answer to part (a) by showing that, approximately,
$$
E \approx E_{o}\left[1-\left(E_{o} / 2 M c^{2}\right)\right] \text {, where } E_{o} / 2 M c^{2} \ll 1 \text {. }
$$

$(\mathbf{c}, \mathbf{5})$ Consider an atom decaying from the excited state $S^{\prime}$ to the ground state $S$, where $M c^{2}$ $=2 \times 10^{11} \mathrm{eV}$ and $E_{o}=4 \times 10^{5} \mathrm{eV}$. A photon energy corresponds to a radiation frequency. Thus the energy difference $E_{o}-E$ corresponds to a frequency shift $\Delta f=f_{o}-f$. Calculate the fractional frequency shift $\Delta f / f_{o}$.
(d, 5) Another way to produce a frequency shift is the Doppler effect, produced by relative motion between source and observer. The atom of part (c) emits the photon energy $E$, which corresponds to light frequency $f$. With what speed and in what direction would an observer have to move relative to this photon, to Doppler-shift its frequency back to the value $f_{o}$ corresponding to the energy $E_{o}$ ? The observer's speed $v$ is $\ll c$.
