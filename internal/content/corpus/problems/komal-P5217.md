The radioactive carbon-14 isotope is constantly produced in the atmosphere by the cosmic rays. Nonetheless, the amount of ${}^{14}\mathrm{C}$ on the Earth is constant, because at a height of 5-9 km, as a result of the reaction $\mathrm{n}+{}^{14}\mathrm{N}~\rightarrow~
{}^{14}\mathrm{C}+\mathrm{p}$, an average of $17\,600$ ${}^{14}\mathrm{C}$ isotopes are produced in each second on each square metre – projected to the surface of the Earth. The half-life of the isotope is 5730 years. 
 Estimate how many tons of ${}^{14}\mathrm{C}$ are present on the Earth. 
 (4 pont)
