Q3
An element A is radioactive, with decay constant $\lambda_{1}$, and decays to element B . B decays, with decay constant $\lambda_{2}$, where $\lambda_{2} \gg \lambda_{1}$, to the stable element C . At time $t$ the number of atoms of A is $N_{1}(t)$ and the number of atoms of B is $N_{2}(t) . N_{0}$ is the total number of atoms. The ratio $R=\left(N_{2} / N_{0}\right)$ can be shown to be given by

$$
R=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\left(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}\right)}\left[e^{-\lambda_{1} t}-e^{-\lambda_{2} t}\right]
$$

a) Sketch a graph of $R$ as a function of $t$.
b) Obtain the approximate variation of $R$ with $t$ for:
(i) $t$ is small
(ii) $t$ large
c) Using extensive experimental data, from measured values of $R$ and $t$, how would you graphically determine:
(i) $\quad \lambda_{1}$ ?
(ii) $\quad \lambda_{2}$, knowing $\lambda_{l}$ ?
d) From the equation for $R$ above deduce the approximate relationship

$$
N_{2} \lambda_{2}=N_{1} \lambda_{1}
$$

State the conditions under which it is valid.
e) Sketch the behaviour of $N_{3}(t)$, the number of C atoms, against $t$.
