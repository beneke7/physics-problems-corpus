Question 11
Zara and Jackie are excited about their new desk chairs. Zara plans to sit and do physics in comfort. Jackie plans to move around on a chair without touching the ground or anything else. "But that's physically impossible!", says Zara. Jackie replies, "No! I've done it before. I lean forward really slowly, and then lean back as fast as I can." Jackie shows Zara how to do it, and she wants to understand it.

Zara calls Jackie's body from the waist up Object 1, and Jackie's legs, from the waist down, and the chair combined Object 2. Zara models the fast part of Jackie's motion using the principle of conservation of momentum. In other words, she thinks that the total momentum of Jackie and the chair (Object 1 + Object 2) remains unchanged while Jackie leans backwards. (Hint: momentum $p=m v$ )

Zara identifies three important times in her model

- $t_{1}$ when Jackie is stationary just before leaning back,
- $t_{2}$ when Jackie is in the process of leaning back, and
- $t_{3}$ when Jackie's back hits the back of the chair.

a) (i) By applying the principle of conservation of momentum, complete the copy of the table above which is on p. 2 of the Answer Booklet. Show your reasoning below the table.
    (ii) Explain why, in Zara's model, the position of the centre of mass of Jackie and the chair never changes, regardless of how far or fast Jackie moves.

b) (i) Describe the motion of Objects 1 and 2 as Jackie leans forward very slowly.
    (ii) Give an explanation of Jackie's motion while leaning very slowly, using ideas from physics.

d) Sketch the positions of Object 1, Object 2, and also the centre of mass position of Jackie and the chair through one cycle of leaning forward very slowly and then leaning back rapidly. Use the axes on p. 3 of the Answer Booklet.
