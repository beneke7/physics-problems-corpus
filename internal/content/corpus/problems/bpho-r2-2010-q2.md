## Q2 This question is about cold dark matter.

Cold dark matter only affects ordinary matter by gravitational interaction. It will deflect light by gravitational interaction. Electromagnetic quanta are thought to rarely interact with cold dark matter, therefore "physical collisions" occur very infrequently. No verified direct detection appears to have yet been recorded and its existence is inferred through indirect observations. These include the deflection of light by invisible bodies and optical effects such as gravitational lensing; double images caused by the gravitational fields and the inferred mass of a group of galaxies rotating about a common centre. In the last case the total mass of the visible galaxies is far less than the total mass needed to produce stable orbits given the observed orbital speeds and orbital parameters. Part (a) illustrates the bending of light rays by mass.
(a) According to the General Theory of Relativity the gravitational field of the Sun alters the effective refractive index, $n$, of outer space so that:

$$
n^{2} \approx 1+\frac{4 a}{r},
$$

where $a$ is a constant which, for the Sun, takes a value of $1.48 \times 10^{3} \mathrm{~m}$ and $r$ is the distance to the centre of the Sun.
