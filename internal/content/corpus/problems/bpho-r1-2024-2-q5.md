## Question 5

This question is about gravity and collisions.

a) Consider a planet of mass $m_{\mathrm{p}}$, orbiting a star of mass $M_{\mathrm{s}}$ in a circular orbit of radius $r$ which takes a time $T$ to complete one orbit.
    (i) By balancing centripetal and gravitational forces, show that the orbital speed $v \propto$ $r^{-1 / 2}$.
    (ii) Hence, show that the total energy of the planet (KE + GPE), $E_{\text {total }} \propto r^{-1}$.
    (iii) By considering the speed of a planet in its orbit around the planet in terms of $r$ and $T$, show that the relationship between orbital period and orbital radius is $T^{2} \propto r^{3}$. (This is known as Kepler's Third Law.)
    (iv) Given the Earth has an orbital radius $r_{\mathrm{E}}=1.50 \times 10^{11} \mathrm{~m}$ and orbital period $T_{\mathrm{E}}=$ 365 days, calculate the mass of the Sun in kg.

Earth's orbital properties can be given in different units, namely $T_{\mathrm{E}}=1$ year, and $r_{\mathrm{E}}=$ 1 au. (An "au" stands for astronomical unit).


(v) Using your result from part (iii), if you use the Earth values of $r_{\mathrm{E}}=1 \mathrm{au}$ and $T_{\mathrm{E}}=1$ year orbiting a star of mass $M_{\mathrm{s}}=1 M_{\text {Sun }}$, what is the value (and units) of $G$ in just these given quantities and units?

For the following calculations, you may choose to work in standard SI units like metres, kilograms and seconds (and so use your value of $M_{\text {Sun }}$ from part (iv)), or in au, solar masses and years, as that can often greatly simplify the formulae (and so use your value of $G$ from part (v)).

b) Disaster strikes and the Sun disappears! This causes all the planets to be perturbed from their orbits. In this question we will just look at Venus and Earth. Assume that the loss of the Sun's gravity was felt instantaneously by both planets, and that both are travelling in coplanar circular orbits.
Venus has a mass of $0.815 M_{\text {Earth }}$, where $M_{\text {Earth }}=5.97 \times 10^{24} \mathrm{~kg}$, and an orbital radius of 0.723 au.
Venus and Earth happened to be at such a point in their orbit when the Sun disappeared that they are on a collision course.
    (i) Calculate the orbital speeds of Earth and Venus, $v_{\mathrm{E}}$ and $v_{\mathrm{V}}$.
    (ii) Determine the time, $t$, until they crash. Give your answer in days.
    (iii) Show that the distance $d$ from the Sun's original location when they crash is ~ 1.5 au.
    (iv) Show that the angle between their velocity vectors as they approach is ~ 13°.
    (v) Find the Earth-Sun-Venus angle at the moment the Sun disappeared in order for this crash to occur.

c) The collision between them is inelastic, causing them to stick together and make a new, heavier planet: "Venearth".
    (i) Show that the speed of Venearth, $v$, just after the collision is $\sim 6.7$ au year $^{-1}$.
    (ii) Determine the angle between Venearth's velocity vector and Earth's initial velocity vector before the collision.
    (iii) Calculate the percentage of kinetic energy lost in the collision.
d) At the moment of the collision, a new star magically appears in the location that the Sun used to be. Again, assume its gravitational effects are felt instantaneously throughout the Solar System.
    (i) What is the minimum mass of this new star necessary for Venearth to still be bound within the Solar System? Give your answer in units of $M_{\text {Sun }}$.

The new planet Venearth will follow an elliptical orbit whose orbital period will be equal to a circular orbit with the same total energy as Venearth.


(ii) If in fact, the new star has a mass equal to $M_{\text {Sun }}$, what will be the orbital period of Venearth? Give your answer in years.
