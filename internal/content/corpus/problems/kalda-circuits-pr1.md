A uniform wire of cross-sectional area $A_{0}=1 \mathrm{~mm}^{2}$ had a millimetre scale marked on it: an array of streaks with interstreak distance $a_{0}=1 \mathrm{~mm}$ covered the entire length of the wire. The wire was stretched in a non-uniform way, so that the interstreak distance $a$ is now a function of the distance $l$ from one end of the wire (as measured after the stretching), see figure. The new length of the wire is $L=4 \mathrm{~m}$. Using the graph, determine the electrical resistance $R$ of the stretched wire assuming that the resistivity of the wire material is $\rho=1.0 \times 10^{-6} \Omega \mathrm{~m}$. During the stretching, the density of the wire material remains constant.
