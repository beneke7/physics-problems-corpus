## Qu 4. The Pendulum

A mass $m$ is suspended from a frictionless pivot $P$ by a light string of length $l$. It is released from rest at an angle $\theta_{0}$ from the vertical.

(a) Prove, given certain assumptions that you should clearly state, that the time period $\tau$ of the subsequent oscillations is
$$
\tau=2 \pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}} .
$$
(b) By considering the principle of conservation of energy, prove that the angular velocity $\frac{d \theta}{d t}$ at angular displacement $\theta<\theta_{0}$ satisfies
$$
\left(\frac{d \theta}{d t}\right)^{2}=\frac{2 g}{l}\left(\cos \theta-\cos \theta_{0}\right) .
$$
(c) Hence argue that the time period of the subsequent oscillations can be written in integral form as
$$
T=\sqrt{\frac{8 l}{g}} \int_{0}^{\theta_{0}} \frac{d \theta}{\sqrt{\cos \theta-\cos \theta_{0}}}
$$
(d) By introducing an auxillary angle $\phi$ such that
$$
\sin \phi=\frac{\sin (\theta / 2)}{\sin \left(\theta_{0} / 2\right)},
$$
prove that
$$
T=\sqrt{\frac{16 l}{g}} \int_{0}^{\pi / 2} \frac{d \phi}{\sqrt{1-k^{2} \sin ^{2} \phi}},
$$
and express the constant $k$ in terms of $\theta_{0}$. Hint: $\cos \theta=1-2 \sin ^{2}(\theta / 2)$.
(e) By inserting a relevant value for $k$ into the integral expression above, show clearly how the result from part (a) can be recovered.
(f) For a general value of $\theta_{0}$ (and therefore $k$ ) there is no closed-form solution for the integral above. However, the result can be expressed in terms of the arithmetic-geometric mean function, agm:
$$
T=\frac{\tau}{\operatorname{agm}\left[\cos \left(\theta_{0} / 2\right), 1\right]},
$$
where $\tau$ is the result derived in part (a). The arithmetic-geometric mean function agm is recursively defined as the large $n$ limit of two simple sequences $a_{n}, b_{n}$ : to find $\operatorname{agm}[a, b]$, set $a_{0}=a, b_{0}=b$ and then:
$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{n+1} & =\frac{1}{2}\left(a_{n}+b_{n}\right) \\
b_{n+1} & =\sqrt{a_{n} b_{n}}
\end{aligned}
$$
for $n=0,1,2, \ldots$. If $\ell=1.00 \mathrm{~m}$ and the mass is released from the same level as the pivot, with the string taut, determine the oscillatory time period to 3 significant figures.
(g) A more complex pendulum is composed of two masses, each $m / 2$, respectively at distances $\ell(1-\alpha)$ and $\ell(1+\alpha)$ from the pivot, where $0<\alpha<1$. The masses are connected with the pivot and to one another by co-linear, light rods. Draw the arrangement and explain, for a given value of $\theta_{0}$, whether the oscillatory time period is shorter than, equal to, or longer than the simple pendulum of mass $m$ and length $\ell$.
