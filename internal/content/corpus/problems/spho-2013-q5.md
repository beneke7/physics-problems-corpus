5. A "perfect" rocket engine combines matter and antimatter in a controlled way to yield photons (high energy gamma rays) all of which are directed out of a spaceship. Suppose we start with a spaceship of initial mass $M_{0}$ at rest, and that at burnout, the remaining spaceship moves at speed $v$ and has mass $m$ equal to the fraction $f$ of the original mass. You may take the speed of light $c=1$ and $\gamma=1 / \sqrt{1-v^{2}}$.
(i) What is the total energy of this system initially? Let $E_{r a d}$ stand for the total energy of radiation after the burnout. Find an expression for the total energy of the system after the burnout and set up the conservation of energy equation.
(ii) Write down the equation for the conservation of momentum.
(iii) Show that $\gamma f+\gamma v f=1$.
(iv) Show that $\gamma v=\sqrt{\gamma^{2}-1}$ so that $f^{2}-2 \gamma f+1=0$.
(v) What is the value of $f$ if $\gamma=10$ ? Is it possible to construct a spaceship whose shell and payload with this $f$ ?
(vi) Using $f$ obtained above for $\gamma=10$, show that the total energy of the emitted radiation is less than the mass of fuel consumed. Explain why.
