A vessel in the shape of a cylinder, whose height equals its radius $R$ and whose cavity is half-spherical, is filled to the brim with water, turned upside down and positioned on a horizontal surface. The radius of the half-spherical cavity is also $R$ and there is a little hole in the vessel's bottom. From below the edges of the freely lying vessel some water leaks out. How high will the remaining layer of water be, if the mass of the vessel is $m$ and the water density is $\rho$ ? If necessary, use the formula for the volume of a slice of a sphere (see Fig.): $V=\pi H^{2}(R-H / 3)$.
