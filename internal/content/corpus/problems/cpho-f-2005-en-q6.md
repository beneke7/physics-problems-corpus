The figure shows a fixed cylindrical shaft $OO'$ with radius $a_1$ and negligible resistance. A homogeneous conducting disk with inner and outer radii $a_1$ and $a_2$, respectively, and thickness $h$ ($h \ll a_1$) is put on the shaft $OO'$, can freely rotate around it, and has good electrical contact with it. The resistivity of the disk at a distance $r$ from its center is proportional to this distance, i.e. $\rho = \rho_0 r$, where $\rho_0$ is some constant. The system is in a magnetic field of induction $B$, perpendicular to the surface of the disk. The figure also shows a constant current source S, which provides a current of magnitude $I$, regardless of the load on it. In parallel with the load, the current source is connected to a resistor with resistance $R_0$. One end of the source is connected to the center of the end surface of the shaft X, and the other end is connected to the ring brush Y. The ring brush surrounds the disc and maintains good electrical contact with it without interfering with its rotation. This system can be used as a motor. When there is no load on it, then the angular velocity of the steady rotation of the disk is $\omega_0$. When there is some load on the motor, the angular velocity is $\omega$. Ignore friction.

![Conducting disk on shaft $OO'$ in field $B$, current source S with resistor $R_0$, contacts X and ring brush Y](../../sources/cpho-f/figures/cpho-f-2005-en-q6-fig1.png)

(a) Find the ratio $\omega/\omega_0$ at which the motor produces the maximum mechanical power $P$.

(b) What thermal power $P_d$ is released in this case on the disk?
