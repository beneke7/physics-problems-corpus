1. A well-oiled machine of some kind (see figure) has a weight (of mass $m$ ) attached with a pivot through its centre of mass (at height $h$ above the ground) to two rigid rods (of equal length $L$ ) with negligible mass.
The rods are pivoted (like an elbow joint) at both ends. One rod has its bottom end pivoted to a fixed pivot on the floor, while the other rod has its bottom end pivoted to a rolling pivot that can move along a straight horizontal track.
Denote the local gravitational field strength as $g$.
    (a) Find the horizontal force $F$ that has to be applied at the rolling pivot to keep the machine in equilibrium. [Express your answer as a function of the variables introduced.]
    (b) Suppose now that the rolling pivot is moving at constant speed $u$ towards the fixed pivot.
        i. State the horizontal speed of the weight.
        ii. State the direction of the acceleration $a$ of the weight, and briefly explain why the horizontal force $F$ that now has to be applied at the rolling pivot is different from your answer in (a).
        iii. By considering the circular trajectory of the weight, find its tangential speed $v$.
        iv. Find the radial acceleration $a_{r}$ of the weight, taking the origin as the fixed pivot, and hence find the acceleration $a$ of the weight.
        v. Find the resultant force on the weight, and hence find the horizontal force $F$ that now has to be applied at the rolling pivot.
