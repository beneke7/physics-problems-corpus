## Hint

Determine $n \lambda$ for the composite line and consider possible values of $n$, the order of the diffraction spectrum.

## Solution

$n \lambda=d \sin \theta=\left(10^{-3} \mathrm{~m} / 300\right) \sin 24.46^{\circ}=1380 \mathrm{~nm}$ and the only possible values for $n$ and $\lambda$ to put the red and blue/violet light into the appropriate parts of the spectrum are $n_{\mathrm{R}}=2, \lambda_{\mathrm{R}}=690 \mathrm{~nm}$ and $n_{\mathrm{BV}}=3$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{BV}}=460 \mathrm{~nm}$. In all physically possible cases

$$
n \lambda \leq d \sin 90^{\circ}=3333 \mathrm{~nm}
$$

and the only other pair of integers which are in the ratio $3 m: 2 m$, with $m$ less than $(3333 / 1380)=2.4$, is 6 and 4 . Thus there is only one more angle
at which a two-component line will be observed; i.e. at

$$
\sin ^{-1} \frac{(6 \times 460)}{3333}=55.9^{\circ} .
$$
