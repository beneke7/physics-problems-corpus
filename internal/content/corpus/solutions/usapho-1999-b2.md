B2. a. Using Faraday's Law

$$
\mathcal{E}=-\frac{d \Phi}{d t} \quad \text { with } \quad \Phi=B A
$$

Let $\theta$ be the angle the magnetic field boundary makes with spoke $c d$ shown in the diagram below. Then

$$
A=\left(\frac{\theta}{2 \pi}\right) \pi r_{o}^{2}=(1 / 2) \theta r_{o}^{2}
$$

And

$$
\mathcal{E}=-\frac{d}{d t}\left(B(1 / 2) \theta r_{o}^{2}\right)=-(1 / 2) B r_{o}^{2} \omega .
$$

This is the emf induced in the circuit branch $c d$. By symmetry the currents to the left of $c d$ equal those to the right of $c d . \mathrm{I}_{5}=0$. Since no current branches off at $b, \mathrm{~L}_{4}=\mathrm{I}_{3}=\mathrm{I}$. The potential drop between point points $a$ and $c$ must be independent of path, so

$$
R I_{2}=R I+R I \quad \text { and } \quad I_{2}=2 I
$$

At point $a$ the total current in must equal the total current out, so And finally at point $d$

$$
\begin{gathered}
I_{1}=2 I+I=3 I \\
I_{0}=3 I+3 I=6 I
\end{gathered}
$$

For loop acda

$$
\mathcal{E}=-(1 / 2) B r_{n}^{2} \omega=6 I R+3 I R+2 I R=11 I R
$$

Or

$$
I=\frac{1}{22} \frac{B r_{o}^{2} \omega}{R}
$$

with the current directions and magnitudes in terms of I shown in the above diagram.
b.

$$
\begin{gathered}
P=\sum I^{2} R=R\left\{(6 I)^{2}+2(3 I)^{2}+2(2 I)^{2}+4(I)^{2}\right\} \\
P=I^{2} R(36+18+8+4)=66 I^{2} R \\
P=66\left(\frac{1}{22} \frac{B r_{o}^{2} \omega}{R}\right)^{2} R=\frac{3}{22} \frac{B^{2} r_{o}^{4} \omega^{2}}{R}
\end{gathered}
$$

c.

$$
P=\tau \cdot \omega
$$

$\tau$ and $\omega$ are in the opposite direction, so

$$
\tau=-\frac{P}{\omega}=-\frac{3}{22} \frac{B^{2} r_{o}^{4} \omega}{R}
$$

$\tau$ is the only torque acting, so

$$
\alpha=\frac{\tau}{I_{o}}=-\frac{3}{22} \frac{B^{2} r_{o}^{4} \omega}{I_{o} R} .
$$

Its direction is opposite to $\omega$.
