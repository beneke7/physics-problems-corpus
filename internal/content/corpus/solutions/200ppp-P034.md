## Hint

Resolving the tension in the string into radial and tangential components, the direction of the string can be calculated using the dynamical conditions for uniform circular motion.

## Solution

For uniform circular motion the tangential acceleration of the body is zero, but the radial acceleration is $v^{2} / R$, where $v$ is the speed of the body and $R$ is the radius of the circle.

![](../../../../../sources/200ppp/figures/200ppp-117.jpg)
Fig. S34.1

As the radial and tangential components of the gravitational force are $m g \sin \theta$ and $m g \cos \theta$, respectively, as shown in Fig. S34.1, the force $F$
exerted by the string $P Q$ on the bob must have components:

$$
F_{\mathrm{t}}=m g \cos \theta \quad \text { and } \quad F_{\mathrm{r}}=\frac{m v^{2}}{R}-m g \sin \theta .
$$

From these expressions we can deduce the direction of the force $F$, and hence the direction of the string, characterised by $\phi$ :

$$
\cot \phi=\frac{F_{\mathrm{r}}}{F_{\mathrm{t}}}=\frac{v^{2}}{g R \cos \theta}-\tan \theta=\frac{1.83}{\cos \theta}-\tan \theta .
$$

Fig. S34.2

In Fig. S 34.2 corresponding positions of the two ends of the string are shown. The angle $\theta$ increases in $15^{\circ}$ steps and the position of the other end $(Q)$ of the string has been plotted from point $P$ by marking off the length of the string in the direction of the calculated angle $\phi$.
