## Hint

From the figure you can determine the angle not given in the text.

## Solution

The puzzling aspect of the problem is that insufficient data have been given in the text. However, the figure can be used as a source of information. Using a protractor you can measure with sufficient accuracy that the tangent to the fixed end of the rope makes an angle of $30^{\circ}$ with the vertical, as shown in the figure. This means that the tension at the fixed end of the rope is $T=20 \mathrm{~N} / \sin 30^{\circ}=40 \mathrm{~N}$. Similarly, the weight of the rope is equal to the vertical component of the tension there; $m g=T \cos 30^{\circ}=34.6 \mathrm{~N}$, giving the mass of the rope as $m=3.5 \mathrm{~kg}$.
![](../../../../../sources/200ppp/figures/200ppp-152.jpg)

Note. Further information can be obtained from the figure. The centre of gravity of the rope must be vertically above the point $P$, because the lines of action of all three forces acting on the rope must meet at a single point.
