# Question 2 — Energy Levels

## (a)

### (i)

\[
n\lambda = 2\pi r,
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
2n\frac{\lambda}{2}=\text{circumference}.
\]

### (ii)

Newton II gives

\[
\frac{mv^2}{r}=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{e^2}{r^2}.
\]

The energy of the system is

\[
E=\frac12mv^2-\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{e^2}{r}
  =-\frac12\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{e^2}{r}.
\]

### (iii)

Using \(n\lambda=2\pi r\) and \(p=h/\lambda\),

\[
n\frac{h}{p}=2\pi r,
\qquad
n\frac{h}{2\pi}=rp.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
n\hbar=rp,
\qquad \hbar=\frac{h}{2\pi}.
\]

### (iv)

From Newton II,

\[
\frac{mv^2}{r}=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{e^2}{r^2},
\qquad
\frac{p^2r}{m}=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}e^2.
\]

Using \(n\hbar=rp\), we can write

\[
\frac{n^2\hbar^2}{r}=p^2r,
\]

and hence

\[
\frac{n^2\hbar^2}{mr}=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}e^2.
\]

Therefore

\[
r=\frac{4\pi\varepsilon_0 n^2\hbar^2}{me^2}.
\]

For \(n=1\),

\[
r=\frac{\hbar^2}{m\left(\dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\right)e^2}
  =0.53\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm m.
\]

> Source audit: the PDF prints \(r=5.3\ \mathrm{nm}\) immediately afterwards. That is inconsistent with the preceding value, which is \(0.053\ \mathrm{nm}\); the printed line is retained here only as a flagged source typo.

### (v)

Substituting the expression for \(r\) into the energy,

\[
E=-\frac12\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{me^4}{\hbar^2n^2}.
\]

Alternatively, for the \(n=1\) energy level,

\[
p=\frac{h}{\lambda},
\qquad
E=-\frac{p^2}{2m}=-\frac{h^2}{\lambda^2 2m}.
\]

Since \(\lambda=2\pi r\),

\[
E=-\frac{h^2}{4\pi^2r^2 2m}
  =-\frac{\hbar^2}{2mr^2}
  =-13.6\ \mathrm{eV}.
\]

## (b)

### (i)

The source diagram shows two particles of masses \(m_1\) and \(m_2\), separated by \(a=r_1+r_2\), orbiting their centre of mass.

For an isolated system,

\[
m_1r_1=m_2r_2.
\]

The centripetal force on each particle is supplied by the electrostatic attraction, so

\[
m_1r_1\omega^2=\frac{ke^2}{a^2},
\qquad
m_2r_2\omega^2=\frac{ke^2}{a^2},
\]

which gives the same value of \(\omega\) for both particles.

Using \(m_1r_1=m_2r_2\),

\[
\frac{m_1}{m_2}(a-r_2)=r_2,
\qquad
\frac{m_1}{m_2}a=r_2\left(1+\frac{m_1}{m_2}\right).
\]

Hence

\[
a=r_2\left(\frac{m_2+m_1}{m_1}\right)=\frac{r_2m_2}{\mu},
\]

and

\[
\omega^2=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{e^2}{a^3}\frac{m_1}{m_2}\mu.
\]

### (ii)

The angular momentum is \(rp=mr^2\omega\). Thus

\[
n\hbar=(m_1r_1^2+m_2r_2^2)\omega
      =(m_2r_2)(r_1+r_2)\omega
      =m_1r_1a\omega.
\]

Therefore

\[
n\hbar=a\mu\,a\omega=\mu a^2\omega,
\]

so

\[
(n\hbar)^2=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\mu ae^2.
\]

This has the same form as before, with \(m\) replaced by \(\mu\) and \(r\) replaced by \(a\). Hence

\[
E=-\frac12\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{\mu e^4}{\hbar^2n^2}.
\]

For hydrogen, \(\mu\approx m_e\), so the \(n=1\) level remains at \(-13.6\ \mathrm{eV}\), while the \(n=2\) level is \(-3.4\ \mathrm{eV}\).

For tritium, the energy levels differ by only about \(0.02\%\). In the source's estimate,

\[
\frac{m_e-\mu}{m_e}=1-\frac{\mu}{m_e}
\approx\frac{m_e}{3m_p}\approx\frac{1}{6000}.
\]

### (iii)

Since \(\mu<m_e\), the energy levels have a smaller magnitude. For tritium,

\[
\mu=\frac{m_e3m_p}{m_e+3m_p}
\approx m_e\left(1-\frac{m_e}{3m_p}\right).
\]

The levels are therefore closer together for tritium. A lower-frequency photon will cause a transition, and hence tritium corresponds to a longer wavelength. Conversely, hydrogen absorbs a shorter wavelength.
