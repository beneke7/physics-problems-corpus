## Hint

At first sight several parameters seem to be missing. Don't worry about it! Find the equilibrium condition and calculate the electrostatic energy of the system in that situation.

## Solution

Using the notation in the figure, the equilibrium condition for the first ball is

$$
\frac{m g}{F}=\frac{\ell}{x}
$$

where

$$
F=k q Q / x^{2}
$$

is the Coulomb force acting on the first ball and $x$ is the distance between the balls carrying charges $q$ and $Q$.
![](../../sources/200ppp/figures/200ppp-201.jpg)

It is clear that the triangles $A B D$ and $C A E$ are similar, and that consequently

$$
\frac{x}{2}: \ell=h: x .
$$

From the three equations above we can calculate the separation of the charges and the electrostatic energy of the system:

$$
x=k \frac{q Q}{2 m g h} \quad \text { and } \quad E_{\text {electro }}=k \frac{q Q}{x}=2 m g h .
$$

The work done is the sum of the changes in electrostatic and gravitational potential energy,

$$
W=2 m g h+m g h=3 m g h .
$$

It is perhaps surprising that the work done does not depend on either the magnitudes of the charges or the length of the thread.
