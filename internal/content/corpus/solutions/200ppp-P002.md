## Hint

Calculate the maximum possible value of the coefficient of friction if the object is not to stop on the table.

## Solution

The average speed of the object is $\frac{1}{2} \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$. Since it decelerates uniformly, $v_{\text {average }}=\frac{1}{2}\left(v_{\text {initial }}+v_{\text {final }}\right)$, and thus its initial speed cannot be greater than $1 \mathrm{~m} s^{-1}$, (since $v_{\text {final }} \geq 0$ ). It follows that the speed of the body decreases by a maximum of $1 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$ in 2 s . Thus the absolute value of its acceleration $|a|$ is at most $0.5 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$, i.e. $\frac{1}{20}$ times the gravitational acceleration. Therefore, the coefficient of kinetic friction between the object and the table surface cannot be greater than $\frac{1}{20}$. This is much smaller than the coefficients of friction between ordinary materials and therefore it is very likely that the object does not slide, but that all or part of it rolls.
