## Hint

The energy (per unit volume) of the electrostatic field is proportional to the square of the electric field strength and to the dielectric constant of the medium: $W_{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{1}{2} \varepsilon_{0} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}} E^{2}$. The dielectric between the plates of the capacitor decreases the electric field (as a result of its polarisation), and therefore the energy of the system decreases as well. The force acting on the dielectric can be calculated from this change in energy (using the work theorem).

## Solution

(i) Let the distance between the capacitor plates of area $A$ be $d$, and the charge on them be $Q$. Examine the situation when a length $x$ of the insulating sheet has already been slid between the plates of length $\ell$ as shown in the figure.
![](../../../../../sources/200ppp/figures/200ppp-232.jpg)

The capacitance of the plane capacitor can be calculated as that of two capacitors connected in parallel, one filled with dielectric and the other empty:

$$
C=\varepsilon_{0} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}} \frac{A}{d} \frac{x}{\ell}+\varepsilon_{0} \frac{A}{d} \frac{\ell-x}{\ell}=\frac{\varepsilon_{0} A}{\ell d}\left[\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}-1\right) x+\ell\right] .
$$

The total capacitance can be seen to increase linearly with $x$ from $\varepsilon_{0} A / d$ to $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}$ times its initial value. As the charge is constant in the present case the energy of the capacitor $W_{\text {cap }}$ can be written as

$$
W_{\text {cap }}=\frac{Q^{2}}{2 C}=\frac{Q^{2} \ell d}{2 \varepsilon_{0} A\left[\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}-1\right) x+\ell\right]} .
$$

It is clear that the energy of the capacitor decreases as $x$ increases! This means that the work done in sliding the insulator in is negative, i.e. the plates effectively suck in the insulator.

The size of the force involved can be calculated by equating the work corresponding to a small change in $x$ with the resulting change in energy $\left(F \Delta x=\Delta W_{\text {cap }}\right)$, and differentiating the expression for $W$,

$$
F=\frac{\mathrm{d} W}{\mathrm{~d} x}=-\frac{\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}-1\right) Q^{2} \ell d}{2 \varepsilon_{0} A\left[\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}-1\right) x+\ell\right]^{2}} .
$$

The magnitude of the force is greatest at $x=0$, since the denominator in the above expression increases with $x$. It is easy to show that when $x=\ell$, the magnitude of the force has decreased by a factor $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}^{2}$.

> Note. How does the electric field exert a force on the dielectric parallel to the plates? If the electric field between the plates were homogeneous and perpendicular to the plates, and zero outside them (i.e. the usual picture of capacitors), then no force could act on the insulating sheet. The phenomenon is explained by the curvature of the electric field that is inevitably present at the edge of the plates.
(ii) The force acting on the dielectric cannot depend on the interaction of the capacitor with its surroundings. Therefore the previous result would be valid in the constant voltage case if it were not for the fact that the charge on the capacitor changes in accordance with $Q=C V$ as soon as the insulating sheet is placed between the plates. Substituting for the charge (which depends on $x$ ) into the above expression gives

$$
F=-\frac{\varepsilon_{0} A\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}-1\right) V^{2}}{2 \ell d},
$$

i.e. in this case the force acting on the insulating sheet is constant.
An interesting conclusion can be drawn from the expression for the energy of the capacitor at constant voltage,

$$
W_{\text {cap }}=\frac{C V^{2}}{2}=\frac{\varepsilon_{0} A\left[\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}-1\right) x+\ell\right] V^{2}}{2 \ell d} .
$$

This shows that, in this case, the energy of the capacitor increases linearly with $x$, and that the change of energy $\Delta W_{\text {cap }}$ corresponding to a small displacement $\Delta x$ of the insulator is

$$
\frac{\Delta W_{\text {cap }}}{\Delta x}=\frac{\varepsilon_{0} A\left(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}}-1\right) V^{2}}{2 \ell d} .
$$

Apart from the sign, this formula is exactly the same as that for the force acting on the insulating sheet. These results can be summarised in the following way: when the dielectric is slid between the plates, the system does work on it (i.e. pulls it in), while the energy of the capacitor increases by the same amount. This is possible because the energy of the battery decreases by twice this amount during the process. The decrease in stored battery energy occurs because the capacitance of the capacitor (and therefore its charge) increases, and the battery has to supply the additional charge. The calculation of the work done by the battery is left to you the reader.
