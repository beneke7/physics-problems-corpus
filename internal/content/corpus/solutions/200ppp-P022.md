## Hint

No student reading this book should need a hint.

## Solution

For quantitative purposes we assume that the resistances of the bulbs do not depend upon the voltages across them. This is far from accurate, but will give the correct qualitative conclusion. If the (r.m.s.) supply voltage is $V$, the resistance $r_{i}$ of a bulb is $V^{2} / w_{i}$, where $w_{i}$ is the nominal rating of the bulb. When the two bulbs are connected in series across the supply, the (r.m.s.) current drawn is $V /\left(r_{A}+r_{B}\right)$ and the power dissipated in bulb $i(i=A$ or $B)$ is

$$
P_{i}=\frac{V^{2}}{w_{i}}\left[\frac{V}{\left(V^{2} / w_{A}\right)+\left(V^{2} / w_{B}\right)}\right]^{2} .
$$

According to the original agreement ( $w_{A}=w_{B}=100 \mathrm{~W}$ ), both $P_{A}$ and $P_{B}$ should be 25 W . Actually, $P_{A}=8 \mathrm{~W}$ and $P_{B}=32 \mathrm{~W}$, and so $A$ clearly failed his examinations. By comparison, student $B$ might be considered a double winner: he gets 32 W , but pays for only $(8+32) / 2=20 \mathrm{~W}$. On the other hand, 32 W is still a very poor light to study by and $B$ also could well have failed his examinations.
