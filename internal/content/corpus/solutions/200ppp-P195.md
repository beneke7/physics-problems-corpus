## Hint

In the course of this process (Compton scattering), the total energy and momentum of the colliding particles (photons + electrons) remains unchanged. It is convenient to take the rest energy of the electron $E_{0}= m_{\mathrm{e}} c^{2} \approx 510 \mathrm{keV}$ as the unit of energy for the calculations.

## Solution

The energy of a photon of frequency $f$ is $h f$, while its momentum is $h f / c$, where $h$ is Planck's constant and $c$ the velocity of light. The energy of the photon decreases to $h f^{\prime}$ after the collision, and its momentum, of magnitude $h f^{\prime} / c$, is perpendicular to the original momentum.
![](../../../../../sources/200ppp/figures/200ppp-266.jpg)

It follows from momentum conservation that, as shown in the figure, the components of the electron's final momentum are $p_{x}=h f / c$ and $p_{y}=h f^{\prime} / c$. Using the principle of conservation of energy in its relativistic form, $E^{2}= E_{0}^{2}+p^{2} c^{2}$, where $E_{0}$ is the rest-mass energy, we obtain

$$
h f+E_{0}=h f^{\prime}+\sqrt{E_{0}^{2}+\left(p_{x}^{2}+p_{y}^{2}\right) c^{2}} .
$$

The above relationships yield the change in the wavelength of the photon as

$$
\Delta \lambda=\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda=\frac{c}{f^{\prime}}-\frac{c}{f}=\frac{h c}{E_{0}}=\frac{h}{m c} \approx 2.4 \times 10^{-12} \mathrm{~m} .
$$

Note. This interesting quantity with the dimensions of a length-dependent only on the mass of the electron, the speed of light and Planck's constant, i.e. natural constants-is called the Compton wavelength of the electron.
