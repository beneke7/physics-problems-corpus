## Hint

The trickiest part of the solution is to appreciate how frictional forces can balance both Jean Valjean's weight and the reaction forces of the walls at the same time.

## Solution

Fig. S10.1 shows Jean Valjean's location on the wall. Figure S10.2 is a sketch showing his weight ( $m g$ ), the normal reactions of the walls ( $N$ ) and the static frictional forces ( $F_{\mathrm{fr}}$ ) acting on his limbs.

Let the static frictional forces make a common angle $\theta$ with the vertical. The conditions for static equilibrium (see Fig. S10.3) are

$$
m g=2 F_{\mathrm{fr}} \cos \theta \quad \text { and } \quad N=F_{\mathrm{fr}} \sin \theta .
$$

From these equations we can express the normal component, $N$, of the force exerted by the prisoner on the wall whilst climbing as

$$
N=\frac{1}{2} m g \tan \theta .
$$

Thus the total force required, $F$, is given by

$$
F^{2}=N^{2}+F_{\mathrm{fr}}^{2}=\left(\frac{m g}{2}\right)^{2} \frac{1+\sin ^{2} \theta}{\cos ^{2} \theta}
$$

We can also find the minimal force using the inequality

$$
F_{\mathrm{fr}} \leq \mu_{0} N,
$$

from which it follows that

$$
\sin \theta \geq \frac{1}{\mu_{0}} \quad \text { or } \quad \tan \theta \geq \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{0}^{2}-1}}
$$

where $\mu_{0}$ is the coefficient of static friction. Using either of these inequalities we find the minimal force to be

$$
F_{\min }=\frac{m g}{2} \sqrt{\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}+1}{\mu_{0}^{2}-1}}
$$

This expression shows that the coefficient of static friction must be greater than unity if Jean Valjean is not to fall off the wall. If the coefficient of static friction approaches infinity, the force on each of his hands is equal to half of his body weight; this situation corresponds to his being glued to the wall.
