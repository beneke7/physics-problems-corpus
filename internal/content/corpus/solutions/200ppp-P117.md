## Hint

Examine the electric field due to a rod element which subtends an angle $\Delta \alpha$ at the point $C$.

## Solution

It follows from the similarity of triangles $F H C$ and $G K H$ in Fig. S117.1, that for the short length of rod $K H$ determined by the small angle $\Delta \alpha$, the following equalities hold:

$$
\Delta x=G H \frac{r}{h}=r \Delta \alpha \frac{r}{h}=\frac{r^{2}}{h} \Delta \alpha .
$$

![](../../../../../sources/200ppp/figures/200ppp-204.jpg)
Fig. S117.1

There is a charge $\Delta Q=\sigma \Delta x$ on $K H$, where $\sigma$ is the charge per unit length, and the magnitude of the electric field strength corresponding to this charge is

$$
\Delta E=\frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0}} \frac{\Delta Q}{r^{2}}=\frac{1}{4 \pi \varepsilon_{0}} \frac{\sigma}{h} \Delta \alpha .
$$

This quantity is independent of the value of angle $\alpha$ itself. It only depends on the angle $\Delta \alpha$ which the piece of rod subtends at $C$. Thus the electric field
vectors due to the lengths of charged rod situated symmetrically with respect to the bisector of angle $C$ are of the same magnitude and their resultant points in the direction of the bisector (see Fig. S117.2).

![](../../../../../sources/200ppp/figures/200ppp-205.jpg)
Fig. S117.2

For any given point $C$ there is some particular value of $\alpha$ such that $A \hat{C} B=2 \alpha$, and by superimposing the results for matched pairs of elementary lengths of the rod, the stated result is established.
