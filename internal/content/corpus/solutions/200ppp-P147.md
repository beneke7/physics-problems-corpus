## Hint

Consider entropy from the point of view of the number of microstates available.

## Solution

The number of microstates available to a system of $N$ objects confined to a space of volume $V$ is proportional, both classically and quantum mechanically, to $V^{N}$. In the current case, initially there are $2 N_{A}$ molecules of helium occupying $2 V_{0}\left(V_{0} \approx 0.0224 \mathrm{~m}^{3}\right)$, and correspondingly for the oxygen. After the partition is removed there are $5 N_{A}$ molecules occupying $5 V_{0}$ and the ratio of the new number of microstates to that before is

$$
\frac{W_{2}}{W_{1}}=\frac{(5 V)^{2 N_{A}}}{(2 V)^{2 N_{A}}} \times \frac{(5 V)^{3 N_{A}}}{(3 V)^{3 N_{A}}}=\frac{(5 V)^{5 N_{A}}}{(2 V)^{2 N_{A}}(3 V)^{3 N_{A}}} .
$$

Thus the change in entropy is

$$
\Delta S=k \ln \frac{W_{2}}{W_{1}}=k N_{A}(5 \ln 5-2 \ln 2-3 \ln 3)=27.9 \mathrm{~J} \mathrm{~K}^{-1} .
$$

Note. If we had investigated the mixing of two identical gases (e.g. two moles of helium with three moles of helium) both at s.t.p., the calculated entropy change would seem to be the same. However, it is obvious there can be no entropy change, because physically nothing happens then. This is called the Gibbs paradox. The resolution of this paradox depends upon the indistinguishability of identical particles.
