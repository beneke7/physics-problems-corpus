A4. (a) The mass of a spherical shell of radius $r$ and thickness $d r$ is

$$
\rho(r) 4 \pi r^{2} d r=A r^{n} 4 \pi r^{2} d r=A r^{n-2} 4 \pi d r
$$

To find the total mass enclosed by a sphere of radius $R$, we add up the mass of all of the spherical shells:

$$
M(R)=\int_{0}^{R} A r^{n+2} 4 \pi d r=\frac{4 \pi A R^{n+3}}{n+3}=C R^{n+3}
$$

where $C=\frac{4 \pi A}{n+3}$.
(b) Gauss's Law applied to a spherical shell of radius R gives us

$$
\oint \vec{g} \cdot d \vec{A}=-4 \pi G M_{\text {enclosed }}
$$

By symmetry, $g$ is constant over the surface of the spherical shell. $g$ is radially inward, so

$$
\oint \dot{g} \cdot d \vec{A}=-g 4 \pi R^{2}
$$

When we substitute (A4-5) into (A4-4) and substitute (A4-2) for $M_{\text {enclosed }}$, we obtain

$$
-g 4 \pi R^{2}=-4 \pi G C R^{n+3}
$$

Solving for g ,

$$
g=G C R^{n+1}
$$

The gravitation force on a star of mass $m$ is then

$$
F_{g r o v}^{\prime}=m g=m C C R^{n-1}
$$

(c) Applying Newton's second law to the circular motion of the star, we find that

$$
F_{g v+1}=\frac{m v^{2}}{R}
$$

Substituting (A4-8) into (A4-9) yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
& m G C R^{n+1}=\frac{m v^{2}}{R} \\
& v=\sqrt{G C R^{n-2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

(d) If $v$ is constant, then $R^{n+2}=R^{0}$, so $n=-2$.
(e) Apply Gauss's Law to a spherical shell of radius $r$, where $r>R$. Here, the mass enclosed is $M_{0}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
& -g 4 \pi r^{2}=-4 \pi G M_{0} \\
& g=\frac{G M_{D}}{r^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting into (A4-9)

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{m G M_{0}}{r^{2}}=\frac{m v^{2}}{r} \\
& v=\sqrt{\frac{G M_{3}}{r}}
\end{aligned}
$$
