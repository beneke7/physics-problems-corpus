## Hint

Apply the (relativistic) conservation laws of energy and momentum.

## Solution

The general equations of conservation of energy and momentum for Compton scattering are:

$$
\begin{gathered}
h f_{0}+m_{0} c^{2}=h f+m c^{2}, \\
\frac{h f_{0}}{c}=\frac{h f}{c} \cos \alpha+p \cos \beta, \\
\frac{h f}{c} \sin \alpha=p \sin \beta, \\
\left(m c^{2}\right)^{2}=\left(m_{0} c^{2}\right)^{2}+p^{2} c^{2},
\end{gathered}
$$

where $f_{0}$ and $f$ denote the frequencies of the incident and scattered photon respectively, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the photon and electron scattering angles (see figure) and $m_{0}$ is the rest mass of the electron.
![](../../sources/200ppp/figures/200ppp-265.jpg)

For the special case considered,

$$
h f_{0}=m_{0} c^{2} \quad \text { and } \quad \frac{h f}{c}=p .
$$

Solving this system of equations we find that

$$
\alpha=\beta=\cos ^{-1} \frac{2}{3} \approx 48.2^{\circ}
$$

and so the angle between the scattered photon and recoil electron is $\alpha+\beta \approx 96.4^{\circ}$. The momentum of the scattered electron is

$$
p=\frac{3}{4} m_{0} c=\frac{m_{0} v}{\sqrt{1-v^{2} / c^{2}}}
$$

yielding the speed of the electron as $v=\frac{3}{5} c$. This is of the same order of magnitude as $c$, and consequently it was appropriate to use relativistic formulae.
