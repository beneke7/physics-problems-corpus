## Hint

The key phrase of the solution is 'in parallel'.

## Solution

Let us denote the equivalent resistances of the original grid by $R_{\mathrm{e}}^{\text {orig }}$ and of the truncated circuit by $R_{\mathrm{e}}^{\text {trun }}$. We can consider the original grid as a circuit of two resistors connected in parallel; the two resistors are $R_{\mathrm{e}}^{\text {trun }}$ and $R$, the latter being the removed resistor. It is then easy to write an equation which involves $R_{\mathrm{e}}^{\text {trun }}$, namely,

$$
R_{\mathrm{e}}^{\text {orig }}=\frac{R \times R_{\mathrm{e}}^{\text {trun }}}{R+R_{\mathrm{e}}^{\text {trun }}} \quad \text { or } \quad R_{\mathrm{e}}^{\text {trun }}=\frac{R \times R_{\mathrm{e}}^{\text {orig }}}{R-R_{\mathrm{e}}^{\text {orig }}} .
$$

For example, in the case of the infinite two-dimensional square grid the equivalent resistance between two neighbouring grid points is $R_{\mathrm{e}}^{\text {orig }}=R / 2$. It follows that the equivalent resistance of the truncated circuit is $R_{\mathrm{e}}^{\operatorname{trun}}=R$.
