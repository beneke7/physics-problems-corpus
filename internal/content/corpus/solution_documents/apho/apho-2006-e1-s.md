     Measurement of the specific heat of aluminum in the 450 C– 650 C
                    temperature range (10 points).


1a) [1.0 point] The heat capacity of water in the first experiment is

                                       C1 = cw m1                                                (1.1)

      where cw= 4,2 kJ/(kg K) is the specific heat of water and т1 is the mass of water
      in the experiment. The heat capacity of water with the aluminum cylinder
      immersed in it:
                                     C 2 = c w m2 + c Al m                                       (1.2)
      where т2 is the mass of water in the experiment, cAl is the specific heat of
      aluminum and т is the mass of aluminum cylinder. The ratio of heat capacities
      is K = C1 / C2 . Then, specific heat capacity of aluminum is determined by the
      formula
                                              m1 − K ⋅ m2
                                     c Al =               cw .                                   (1.3)
                                                 K ⋅m


The ratio of heat capacities К can be determined from the experiment in 1b) and 1c).
To be able to extract K from these two experiments, one should perform the
measurements in the regime such that the level of water is the same for both
experiments. This can be done by marking the level of the water on the side of the
cup by usual pen, or by choosing the masses of water such that the equation
                                      m1          m2       m
                                              =        +
                                      ρw          ρw       ρ Al
is closely satisfied. Since (1.3) have the difference in the numerator, best results are
obtained when m1 is chosen to be close to m.

      t 2 = 7.8 min . t1 is set by the moment of immersion, t2 is determined as described
