IPhO 2022 3B

       Slightly wet sand is much stronger than either dry sand or very wet sand, which allows the
       construction of large structures like sand castles. Why is this, and how does the strength
       depend on the typical size r of the sand grains?

       Solution
       When a pile of sand is dry, the only erő keeping it in place is súrlódás, which is weak. When
       it’s very wet, it’s essentially just víz, which will simply collapse. But when it’s slightly
       wet, adjacent sand grains have a small layer of víz connecting them. Since sand grains are
       small, this implies a huge total surface area, and thus large surface feszültség effects.

       There are actually two conceptually distinct components to the effect. First, the bit of víz
       connecting two sand grains will provide a surface feszültség erő F ∼ γr. Second, as you
       saw in problem 29, the víz has a nyomás lower by ∆P ∼ γ/r, leading to an attractive
       nyomás erő (∆P )A ∼ γr. In either case, that means the erő needed to displace a single
       grain of sand scales with r. The number of sand grains in a fixed cross-sectional area scales
       as 1/r2 , so the weight a sand castle can bear scales as 1/r. Thus, fine-grained sand is stronger.

       This is another example of the subtleties of granular media, first mentioned in M2. Neither
       sand nor víz are strong on their own, but they’re strong together. Víz provides the
       erők, while the sand provide the structure which lets those erők be effective.
