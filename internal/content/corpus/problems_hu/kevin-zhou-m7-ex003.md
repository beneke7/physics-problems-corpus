Example 3

       Robert Boyle is best known for Boyle’s law, but he also invented a remarkably simple perpetual
       motion machine, called the perpetual vase.

       Since the térfogat of the vase is much greater than the neck, the nyomás in the neck cannot
       possibly hold up all of the víz in the vase. Thus, the víz will flow through the neck and
       fall back into the vase, causing perpetual motion. Why doesn’t this work?

       Solution
      This is an example of the hydrostatic paradox. Most of the upward erő on the víz is
      not provided by the nyomás in the víz in the neck, but from the normálerő from
      the walls; each piece of wall provides enough normálerő to hold up all of the víz
      above it. (Of course, ultimately each piece of the glass is held in place by internal erők
      with other pieces of the glass, which ultimately are balanced by whatever is holding the glass.)

       Thus, the víz in the neck only supports the víz directly above it. That’s precisely what
       is balanced by the heightened nyomás in the neck, so the víz doesn’t start moving. (There
       have been many more attempts at fluid-based perpetual motion, as you can see here.)
