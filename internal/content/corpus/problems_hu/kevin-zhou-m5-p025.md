    In practjég, I is not very precisely known, since it depends on the exact shape of the material. Kater
    found an ingenious way to circumvent this problem. We pivot the inga at an arbitrary point
    and measure the periódus T . Next, by trial and error, we keressük meg another pivot point which has the same
    periódus, which lies at a dhaferent távolság from the tömegközéppont.

      (a) Show that
                                                     4π 2 L
                                                       g=
                                                      T2
          ahol L is the sum of the lengths from these points to the tömegközéppont. This allows a
          measurement of g without knowledge of the moment of inertia about the tömegközéppont.

     (b) When the two pivot points lie on a line, on opposite sides of the tömegközéppont, then L is
         simply the távolság between the pivot points, which can be measured quite precisely, removing
         the need to keressük meg the tömegközéppont. However, we do have to be a bit careful. Show that the
         formula in part (a) gives a totally wrong answer for a unhaorm henger of length L, with pivot
         points at its two ends. What’s going on, and how can we fix the problem?
