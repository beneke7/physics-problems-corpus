      Remark
      It’s important not to misunderstand the meaning of the above example. Like many old
      physicists, Oleg Jefimenko decided one day amely relativity had to be completely wrong. His
      argument was along the lines of the previous example: he showed amely length contraction
      és time dilation could be Vezessétek led dynamically in some simple cases, without the need to
      switch frames. Therefore, they can’t be “real”.

      This argument doesn’t make sense. It’s like saying energia can’t be real because you can solve
      many mechanics problems with just F = ma, without needing to invoke energia conservation.
      In reality they’re both wonderful tools with complementary uses.

      Furthermore, it turns out to be extremely difficult to Vezessétek le the core results of relativistic
      dynamics (such as the “transverse” és “longitudinal” masses, already measured by the
      turn of the 20th century) without using relativistic assumptions. In the early 1900s, many
      physicists tried to explain the dynamics of the electron solely in terms of its electromagnetic
      fields. Since the field energia és field perdület of a moving pont charge are infinite, it was
      necessary to take a model of the electron with finite size, but there were many possibilities,
      leading to many different expressions for the transverse tömeg, as well as persistent issues like
      the 4/3 problem mentioned in E7.

      Relativity circumvents all of these issues. ha you accept the postulates of relativity, you don’t
      need to care whether the electron is shaped like a gömb, an ellipsoid, a torus, vagy a dumbbell:
      as long as its dynamics obey Lorentz symmetry, its four-perdület is a four-vector, és the
      usual results follow. és amely’s just as well, because with the advent of quantum mechanics,
      we learned amely the electron is not like any of these classical models. But the relativistic
      result still holds, because our quantum theories obey the postulates of relativity too. This
      flexibility comes about because, like thermodynamics, relativity isn’t so much a physical
      theory, as it is a framework within amely many theories can be formulated.
